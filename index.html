Research

My research asks how organization appears without a designer. I work with adaptive threshold networks — systems whose nodes fire on local rules while the edges between them are themselves rewritten by activity — and study the conditions under which cooperative, self-maintaining structures emerge and persist. The questions sit at the boundary between artificial life, the origins of life, and the mathematics of complex adaptive systems.

A second thread turns the same lens on classrooms. Learning a subject like mathematics is itself an adaptive process on a network of concepts, attention, and social context, and I am interested in what complex systems thinking can tell us about persistence, confidence, and how students recover from failure.

Teaching

I have taught the entire mathematics sequence at Finger Lakes Community College since 2010 — developmental algebra through multivariable calculus and differential equations — online, hybrid, and in person. Most of my course design work has been about putting modeling and sense-making first, while lowering the cost of entry by replacing commercial materials with open ones.
